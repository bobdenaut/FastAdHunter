54.9 MiB resident · 45 % of the steady-state budget
The peak sits past the 128 MB line and that is not a breach: the budget governs steady state, and this mark is a compile transient the process has not been near since. Both lines are budgets, not limits. Nothing enforces them at runtime — the container runs memory-high=unlimited, so crossing one is a budget breach to investigate, never a kill. They are drawn as markers rather than a red wall for exactly that reason.

The four sum to RSS exactly — residual is process_rss − accounted_bytes, so it is a real slice, not an estimate of what is left over. It is hatched rather than coloured because it is not one structure: binary text and data pages, thread stacks, the Tokio runtime, and memory the allocator holds but has not returned to the kernel. Peak is deliberately not a slice here. A lifetime high-water mark is not part of current RSS — putting 133.7 into this ring would draw a whole that does not exist, and would make the ruleset look half its real share. It belongs on the rail above, which is what a high-water mark is for.

Residual is the number to watch, and only its trend means anything — it is never zero. It swelled to about 70 MiB and came back down while every component stayed flat: that is the allocator holding freed memory and later returning it, not a leak. A leak is residual that rises and never returns. Growth in a component is not a leak either — it is that structure filling toward its cap. And a rising fault rate at flat RSS is purge thrash, not growth.

The hump is residual alone — the three components are flat lines across the whole window. That is the allocator, not the engine. The compile peak never appears in the area, because it lasts seconds against a 60-second sample interval: the sampled series has never exceeded 97 MiB, while the high-water mark caught 133.7. That is the whole reason peak is charted beside the stack instead of derived from it. Cache and stats are hairlines at this scale. That is the true proportion, not a rendering fault — share reads the small bands, separate gives each its own axis.

Minor page faultsthe rate, never the counter

The counter is cumulative since process start, so charting it draws a ramp and says nothing. Its derivative is the purge-thrash detector: a rising rate at flat RSS means the purge delay is returning pages the allocator is about to fault straight back in.

Major faults are structurally near-zero — nothing FastAdHunter touches is demand-paged from disk, so a non-zero value here means host memory pressure, not a FastAdHunter problem. Committed exceeding RSS several times over is expected, not a problem: the allocator never decrements the counter when a purge returns pages, so it only rises. RSS is the authority on footprint. The two committed figures being equal is the normal state — if they ever diverge, the allocator has started accounting purges and the live figure becomes worth reading as one. Both come from whichever allocator is linked in and carry no compatibility promise, which is why they are labelled and parked here rather than charted next to the kernel readings.
